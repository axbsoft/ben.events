Exhibition stands: from design to set-up

A stand has a few seconds to stop a visitor walking down the aisle. Here is how to design and build it, from the brief to the take-down, within the rules of each show.

benevents agency · Published 27 November 2026

What a stand has to do

A stand has three moments to get right. From a distance, it must tell a visitor walking down the aisle who you are. Up close, it must welcome people comfortably, show your products and leave room to talk. And it must look as good on the last day of the show as on the first.

Before drawing anything, decide what visitors should do on the stand: discover a product, book a meeting, leave their details, watch a demonstration. The design follows from that.

The brief for the stand

A good brief fits on one page:

  • The show, its dates, the hall and the stand number.
  • The surface and the type of space: one side open, a corner, a peninsula or an island.
  • The objective and the main messages.
  • The products to display, with their dimensions and weight.
  • How many people will work on the stand, and the meetings to hold: is a table enough, or is a closed space needed?
  • The screens and demonstrations planned.
  • Storage: brochures, gifts, personal belongings.
  • The budget, and whether the stand must be used again at other shows.

Attach the floor plan of the hall: where the stand sits in the aisle, its neighbours and the entrances all change the design.

The floor plan and the rules of the show

Each organiser has its own rules, gathered in its exhibitor manual or technical guide. Read it as soon as the space is booked: it sets what the stand is allowed to do, and the deadlines not to miss. It usually covers:

  • The maximum height, and the rules for anything hung above the stand.
  • The sides that must stay open, and the setbacks from the neighbours.
  • Requirements for materials, in particular how they behave in a fire.
  • Ordering the electrical connection and, if needed, water or internet.
  • The plans to submit for approval, and their deadline.
  • The set-up and take-down times, and access for contractors.

When a rule is unclear, ask the organiser in writing, before anything is built.

The structure: made to measure or modular

A made-to-measure stand, built in a workshop, allows any shape and any finish. A modular stand, with an aluminium structure and tension-fabric walls, is lighter, quicker to set up, and reusable from one show to the next. The two can be combined: a modular structure for the walls, and made-to-measure pieces for the counter or the display case.

A raised floor hides the cables and marks the edge of the stand. It must stay easy to step onto: a ramp or a low edge avoids putting some visitors off.

Our page Exhibition stands sets out the design, construction and installation we take care of.

Printing and signage

What is seen from afar is what is high up: the logo and a short sentence, placed above the visitors’ heads. The rest is read up close: the products, the arguments, how to get in touch.

Printed fabric stretched in frames (SEG) gives walls without creases, and can be backlit to stay bright under the lights of the hall. Vinyl dresses counters, windows and floors. Files are prepared at real size, with a proof approved for each visual before printing. Our page Printing & signage presents the materials available.

Screens and light on the stand

The light in an exhibition hall is often flat. Spotlights on the products, a backlit wall or an illuminated logo make the stand catch the eye from the aisle. A screen shows a video loop or a demonstration; if it plays sound, check what the rules allow, and think of the neighbours.

Screens and light depend on the electrical connection ordered from the organiser: work out the power you need before the ordering deadline. For a video wall, our guide to LED screen rental helps to choose the pitch and the size.

Furniture and circulation

Furniture is chosen for what happens on the stand: a counter in your colours with storage, high tables for quick conversations, seating for meetings, displays for the products. Keep the entrance clear: a counter set across the way stops visitors instead of inviting them in.

Plan a closed storeroom, however small, for coats, stock and boxes. And if hostesses welcome visitors on the stand, brief them as for any event: our article on welcoming your guests gives the method.

Set-up, the show and take-down

The set-up days are fixed by the organiser, and often short. The pieces are therefore made and checked in the workshop, then assembled on site. The stand must be ready, cleaned and lit before the opening, after a last round of checks: sockets, screens, light, stability.

During the show, someone must be able to come and deal with a lamp, a screen or a sticker coming loose. The take-down is done within the time allowed, leaving the space empty; what will be used again is packed and stored for the next show.
